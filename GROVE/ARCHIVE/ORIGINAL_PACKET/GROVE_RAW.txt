Here is the new slew.

**METHOD CARD 001 — Source-Ladder Triangulation**

Every node needs at least three source lanes when possible:

Primary/object source: photo, map, program, city record, archive finding aid, newspaper scan, original club publication.
Institutional/history source: archive summary, library catalog, historical article, architectural database.
Reception/comparison source: later article, commentary, symbolic reading, public interpretation.

No node gets promoted just because one source says it. It gets a proof label: locked, likely, candidate, adjacent, or rejected.

**METHOD CARD 002 — Name-Variant Storm**

For every node, search all its possible names, not just the modern name.

Example: Bohemian Club may need searches for “Bohemian Club,” “The Bohemians,” “Jolly Corks,” “High Jinks,” “Midsummer Jinks,” “Grove Play,” “Nest of the Owl,” “Bohemian Club House,” “club-house,” “clubhouse,” “chalet,” “lodge,” “camp,” “circle,” “shrine,” “lake,” and “owl.”

This matters because older records do not use today’s labels consistently.

**METHOD CARD 003 — Place-Chain Reconstruction**

Build the index as a movement chain, not a list of famous places.

For Bohemian, that means:

home meeting → rented room → first formal club rooms → early city headquarters → outdoor picnic route → Grove predecessor site → purchased Grove land → first Grove building/lodge → later city clubhouse → exterior plaque/markers → internal Grove ritual/performance nodes → camps.

This prevents the 1933 plaque from wrongly becoming “first.”

**METHOD CARD 004 — Address Drift Sweep**

Search every known address separately and then search adjacent blocks.

For this project, early pressure points include James Bowman’s home, Jolly Corks, 430 Pine, 130 Post, Stockton/Post, Post/Leavenworth Little Palace, Post/Taylor, and 624 Taylor.

A location is not locked until we know whether it is exact, approximate, renamed, demolished, rebuilt, or confused with a later address.

**METHOD CARD 005 — Archive Box-to-Object Chase**

When an archive says it has programs, menus, posters, scripts, maps, scrapbooks, or photographs, we do not stop at the collection title. We create extraction targets.

The OAC Bohemian Club Collection covers 1872–2009 and includes play and concert programs, posters, fliers, dinner invitations, menus, plays/scripts, correspondence, membership records, notices, and event material from both the city club and Bohemian Grove. It also says the collection has 1873–1912 scrapbooks and a 1910 Summer Encampment publication called *The Nightly Hoot*. ([OAC][1])

That means the index gets a source row for the collection, then later object rows extracted from it.

**METHOD CARD 006 — Photo-Caption Mining**

Photographs are treated as source objects, not decoration.

For every photo collection, extract:

caption, date, location, visible structures, visible text, visible symbols, seating pattern, gate/threshold placement, camp name, photographer if known, and whether the image proves existence or only suggests context.

This is especially important for Grove nodes because signs, stages, camp markers, and shrine details may appear in photos before they appear in text.

**METHOD CARD 007 — Map Grid Extraction**

Maps become tables.

Every map gets split into roads, trails, camps, buildings, circles, stages, water features, named groves, entrances, boundaries, and legends.

The 1952 Grove map/source we already found becomes a major work object later, not just a citation. It should produce a camp grid and a structure grid.

**METHOD CARD 008 — Date Pressure Crosswalk**

Dates get their own ledger.

For each date: documented event, object placement, repeated appearances, contradiction pressure, and symbolic/riddle pressure.

Example: **1933** cannot be treated only as the plaque date, because one archive line also says women were completely banned from the clubhouse from 1910 to 1933. That may be unrelated, but it is enough to become a watch-date, not a throwaway. ([OAC][1])

**METHOD CARD 009 — Cover / Marker / Riddle Split**

For each object, force four separate boxes:

Cover: what they say it means.
Marker: what it does in place.
Riddle: what contradiction or symbol tension it creates.
Proof: what is actually proven.

This is the card that protects your “don’t just repeat what they say” rule.

**METHOD CARD 010 — Contradiction Harvest**

When a source says something that clashes with the object, do not smooth it out. Capture it.

Example: “Weaving spiders come not here” says no web-weaving, but the institution itself is a powerful network. That is not automatic proof of hidden code, but it is a real riddle pressure point.

**METHOD CARD 011 — Negative-Space Search**

Search for what should exist but is missing.

Examples:

Why is the first meeting address hard to find?
Where are the exact Jolly Corks rooms?
Where is the plaque dedication notice?
Where are building permits or cornerstone records?
Why does one source say 1909, another 1934, and the plaque says 1933?
Are there photos of the old 430 Pine rooms, the Little Palace phase, or the 130 Post clubhouse?

Absence does not prove secrecy. It creates a target.

**METHOD CARD 012 — Adjacent Quarantine**

Masons, Odd Fellows, Elks, owl symbolism, Shakespeare, mystery schools, and fraternal architecture can help with comparison, but they stay quarantined until a source links them.

Comparison is not lineage. Similarity is not proof.

**METHOD CARD 013 — Insider-Word Sweep**

Search their own repeated words.

For Bohemian: owl, spider, weaving, care, jinks, sire, grove, camp, circle, shrine, lake, hoot, midsummer, encampment, play, cremation, nest.

This works because private groups often repeat house-language across programs, menus, maps, speeches, plaques, and room names.

**METHOD CARD 014 — Event-to-Place Conversion**

Events often reveal places.

A “dinner,” “play,” “jinks,” “encampment,” “concert,” “exhibition,” or “banquet” is not only an event. It may identify a room, stage, grove, circle, camp, or marker.

So event records become location leads.

**METHOD CARD 015 — Demolition / Fire / Rebuild Gate**

Every city-club location must be checked against San Francisco fires, earthquake damage, rebuilding, temporary quarters, and address changes.

This already exposed more nodes: Calisphere has a “Bohemian Club headquarters before the fire” result, and another search result points to the Little Palace Hotel at Post and Leavenworth being leased as a Bohemian Club home after the 1906 fire. ([Calisphere][2]) ([The Palace Hotel][3])

**METHOD CARD 016 — Membership-Map Separation**

Members are not the index, but membership records can explain why locations matter.

The OAC collection includes membership records and notices to members, but those should be used to date/place institutional movement, not to turn the index into a people-hunt. ([OAC][1])

**METHOD CARD 017 — Object Family Builder**

For each physical node, search for object families:

plaques, doors, gates, owls, inscriptions, cornerstones, stage backdrops, camp signs, menus, programs, seals, medals, letterheads, maps, tickets, banners, and dedication stones.

This prevents “plaque only” tunnel vision.

**METHOD CARD 018 — Proof-Status Downgrade Rule**

No row stays overpromoted.

Labels:

LOCKED_OBJECT — exact object/place supported.
LOCKED_SOURCE — source exists but objects inside still need extraction.
LIKELY_NODE — strong source, details incomplete.
CANDIDATE_NODE — plausible, needs confirmation.
ADJACENT_ONLY — useful comparison, not part of Bohemian chain.
REJECTED — false lead.

**METHOD CARD 019 — Round-Robin Search Rotation**

Do not search the same way twice in a row.

Each batch rotates through:

archive search, newspaper search, map search, image/caption search, architecture search, catalog search, name-variant search, date search, and contradiction search.

That keeps us from getting trapped inside one source ecosystem.

**METHOD CARD 020 — Batch Closeout Card**

Every batch closes with:

new nodes added, nodes upgraded, nodes downgraded, unresolved address questions, date pressure notes, symbol/riddle pressure notes, best sources found, weak sources rejected, and next batch target.
